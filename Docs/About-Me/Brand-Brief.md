## 2. Differentiator

Most product designers stop at the handoff. Most PMs don't design. Most marketers don't do either.
Sina's resume shows all three in one role — at Digikala the same person defined the product
vision, designed it, ran the zero-fee / installment / gift-card campaigns, built the user
segmentation, and wrote the BI dashboards that tracked NMV, CTR, CPC and conversion (resume).

That hybrid is the brand. Supporting threads:

- **Industrial Design background** (resume: Azad University) → thinks in systems and physical
  constraints, not just screens.
- **Range of industries** — fintech, automotive, retail, edtech, cloud, media, telecom/gaming
  (resume: nine roles). Reads as adaptability, and as pattern-matching across markets.
- **Data literacy** — GA4, Amplitude, Heap, FullStory, Clarity, Hotjar, Tag Manager, Search
  Console (resume: Tools). Design decisions come with instrumentation.
- **Started young** — with ten years of experience at 27, the first roles began around 17.

## 5. Tone of voice

Four traits, drawn from how the resume is written (specific, outcome-first, unpretentious) and
the interests listed in it (adventure, psychoanalysis — curious, reflective).

| Trait | Do | Don't | Example rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct, evidence-led** | Lead with what changed and how you know | Adjectives without outcomes | ~~"Passionate about crafting delightful experiences"~~ → "Replaced the campaign spreadsheets with dashboards; the team stopped arguing about numbers." |
| **Plain language** | Explain the acronym once, then use it | Assume the reader knows NMV or BNPL | "NMV — net merchandise value, what customers actually paid" |
| **Warm, first person** | "I", short sentences, the occasional aside | Corporate third person on the About page | ~~"Sina is a results-driven professional"~~ → "I like the part where a vague business problem turns into something people use." |
| **Curious, reflective** | Say what you learned, including what didn't work | Only wins | "The first segmentation model was wrong about who would buy gold. The second one wasn't." |

> ❓ **Q9 — Confirm:** Do these four traits sound like you? Cut, add or rename. If you have any
> writing you like (LinkedIn posts, a project description), send it and I'll calibrate against it.

Sina Oshaghi designs and manages digital products, and then makes sure they grow. Over ten

Sina trained in industrial design at Azad University and keeps studying interaction design
through the Interaction Design Foundation. Off-screen: motorcycles, mountains, and an
enduring interest in psychoanalysis — which turns out to be useful for user research.

## 8. Visual brand hints

Records only what is **adopted** — candidates stay in [`../Design-System/`](../Design-System/README.md)
(D-013). First sprint adopted on 2026-09-19 (D-015 … D-018), all visible on `/design`:

- **Colour** — one paper, one panel, three inks, two hairlines, one accent with its foreground and
  focus ring (DS-02). Light is a near-neutral warm paper, dark is a true slate — two palettes, not an
  inversion (D-015). The single-accent rule holds: CTA, section tag, live data, nowhere else (DS-01);
  the cobalt is a **placeholder** until Q1 of DS-01 is decided (D-017).
- **Type** — two roles: Geist Sans for reading, Geist Mono for labels and codes; Vazirmatn as the
  Persian partner, switched by `lang` (DS-04, D-016). Sizes are fluid roles (display · h1 · h2 · h3 ·
  lede · body · small · caption · eyebrow · button · num), each with its own leading and tracking;
  Persian takes taller leading, zero tracking, no uppercase and a 13px label floor (DS-03, DS-09).
- **Devices** — two-tone headline (lead in ink, tail muted — DS-08), section opener with the tag on
  the rule (DS-12), mono eyebrow caps and index codes as ornament (DS-09, DS-10), tag chips (DS-22).
- **Rhythm and controls** — fluid section padding and gutters (DS-05), 48px / 36px controls with
  4px radii — a decision, not pleurat's 0px by accident (DS-07, DS-16), one easing
  `cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1)` and a −2px hover lift (DS-06).
- **Patterns** — button pair (DS-16), case-study meta strip with *Company* first (DS-17), metrics
  strip with a source line under every number (DS-18), typographic employer grid instead of a logo
  wall (DS-20), "What shipped" list with a configurable label (DS-27), mobile drawer with numbered
  links and a language switch in its foot (DS-32), editorial two-column content (DS-13).

Every value lives in `Design-System/tokens/sina.tokens.json` and reaches the site through
`pnpm tokens:build` (D-014, D-018). Two constraints stay in force:

- **Bilingual typography** — every type choice must have a Persian counterpart that holds the same
  weight and rhythm, and layouts must mirror cleanly for RTL (logical properties only).
- **Photography exists** — the resume has a portrait; whether the site is photo-led or
  work-led is still a design-benchmark question.
